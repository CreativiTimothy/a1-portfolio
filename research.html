Independent research project

How Perceived Demographics Influence Profile Picture Attractiveness

Demographics such as age, race, gender, and sexuality

Background

I have always been curious whether certain groups of people experienced more prejudice than others based on how they are perceived. Many research studies seem to cover in-person prejudice, but there did not seem to be as much research on virtual platforms, specifically avatar-based livestreaming apps. As someone with over 100,000 followers on an avatar livestreaming app, with extensive experience in technology in spreadsheet software for data analysis, and a psychology background that drives my curiosity to learn more about human behavior, I was well-equipped to conduct my own research study to answer this question.

Research Question

How do demographics such as age, race, gender, and sexuality correlate with perception of profile picture attractiveness?

Can we uncover implicit prejudice through perception of profile picture attractiveness? What groups are most marginalized?

Methods

Participant data was collected through an online survey (Google Form), with data collection and analysis in a Google Spreadsheet. Participants were recruited through my popular livestreams, the Discord server of the livestreaming app (promoted by the developers of the livestreaming app), and word of mouth. This study is a correlational study harnessing spreadsheet calculations to calculate Pearson's r coefficient for each demographic factor (age, race, gender, and sexuality) with profile picture attractiveness.

Results

The study showed that, on average, with a rating scale of 1-10 for attractiveness, profile pictures with the most expensive clothing were rated 2 points higher, profiles perceived as female rated 0.5 points higher than profiles perceived as males, profiles perceived as gay received the lowest ratings, and profiles perceived as Asians received the highest ratings.

Reflection

These findings are generally consistent with research in psychology, except that profiles perceived as Asian, not Whites, were rated most attractive. The unusual preference in Asians can be explained by how the app is run by a Japanese company, and the avatars have facial features aligned with Japanese beauty standards.
